## Intended Users
- Dartmouth faculty, staff, and student organizations
- Communications and marketing teams
- Designers and content creators working on Dartmouth-affiliated materials

## Scope
The skill covers: logo usage, color palettes, typography, photography, iconography, and digital/print design standards as defined by Dartmouth's official brand guidelines.

## Core Brand Identity

Dartmouth's visual identity is rooted in tradition, academic prestige, and a bold, modern sensibility. The brand is built to convey:
- **Heritage** – One of the oldest universities in the US, founded 1769
- **Intellectual rigor** – A leading research and liberal arts institution
- **Community** – A tight-knit, inclusive Ivy League campus culture

The brand voice should feel authoritative yet approachable, timeless yet contemporary.

> **Note:** The assistant should default to recommending the official guidelines page at [communications.dartmouth.edu/design-guidelines](https://communications.dartmouth.edu/design-guidelines) for the most current assets and downloadable resources.

## Logo Usage Rules

### Official Marks
Dartmouth has several official marks including:
- The **Dartmouth wordmark** (primary)
- The **shield/crest** (secondary, formal contexts)
- **Unit/department lockups** (for colleges and offices within Dartmouth)

### Dos
- Use official, approved logo files provided by Dartmouth Communications
- Maintain clear space around the logo at all times
- Use the full-color version on white or light backgrounds
- Use the white reversed version on dark or Dartmouth Green backgrounds

### Don'ts
- Do **not** recreate, redraw, or alter the logo
- Do **not** stretch, rotate, or add effects (shadows, outlines, gradients)
- Do **not** use unofficial or outdated versions of the mark
- Do **not** combine the logo with other logos without approval
- Do **not** place the logo on busy or low-contrast backgrounds

### File Formats
- **Print:** EPS or PDF (vector)
- **Digital:** PNG (with transparency) or SVG
- **Never use:** Low-resolution JPEGs for official communications

- Ensure all color combinations meet **WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility** contrast ratios for digital content

## Typography System

### Primary Typefaces
Dartmouth's brand uses a curated typographic system. Based on their guidelines, the system typically includes:

**Serif (Editorial / Headings)**
- Used for formal communications, headlines, and editorial contexts
- Conveys academic tradition and prestige

**Sans-Serif (Body / Digital)**
- Used for body copy, UI elements, and digital contexts
- Prioritizes legibility at small sizes

> ⚠️ Specific licensed typeface names and weights should be sourced directly from [Dartmouth's brand portal](https://communications.dartmouth.edu/design-guidelines), as font licensing may restrict redistribution details.

### Typographic Hierarchy
1. **Display / Hero** – Large, bold, high-impact headlines
2. **Section Headers** – Clear, structured subheadings
3. **Body Copy** – Readable at 16px+ for digital; 10–12pt for print
4. **Captions / Labels** – Smaller, lighter weight, supplementary info

### Rules
- Do not use more than 2–3 typeface families in a single piece
- Maintain consistent line height and spacing
- Avoid decorative or novelty fonts in official materials
- Use sentence case or title case — avoid ALL CAPS for long passages

## Photography Style

### Tone & Style
Dartmouth photography should feel:
- **Authentic** – Real students, faculty, and campus moments (not overly staged)
- **Aspirational** – Showcasing achievement, curiosity, and community
- **Diverse & Inclusive** – Reflecting the breadth of the Dartmouth community
- **Seasonal & Place-based** – The New Hampshire landscape and campus architecture are key visual assets

### Subject Guidelines
- Prioritize candid, in-context shots over studio portraits
- Show collaboration, research, outdoor life, and intellectual engagement
- Avoid clichéd "stock photo" aesthetics

### Technical Standards
- High resolution (minimum 300 DPI for print; 72 DPI @ 2x for digital/retina)
- Proper white balance and color grading consistent with brand palette
- Green tones in landscape photography should complement Dartmouth Green

### Usage Rights
- Only use images cleared for use by Dartmouth Communications
- Do not use unlicensed stock photography in official materials
- Credit photographers where required

### Photo Don'ts
- Avoid heavily filtered or Instagram-style edits
- Avoid images that feel exclusionary or misrepresent campus culture
- Do not crop official portraits awkwardly

## Digital Design Standards

### Web
- Follow Dartmouth's **web content standards** and CMS templates where available
- Ensure all digital content meets **WCAG 2.1 AA** accessibility standards
- Use responsive design — all layouts must work on mobile, tablet, and desktop
- Consistent navigation and footer patterns should follow the institutional template

### Email
- Use approved Dartmouth email templates from Communications
- Keep email widths at 600px for maximum compatibility
- Include plain-text fallbacks
- Ensure proper alt text on all images

### Social Media
- Use approved profile image/logo treatments for each platform
- Maintain consistent brand voice — professional but warm
- Use brand colors and typography in designed graphics
- Recommended sizes vary by platform (check current platform specs)

### Accessibility
- Color contrast ratio: minimum **4.5:1** for body text (AA)
- All images must have descriptive alt text
- Videos must include captions or transcripts
- Interactive elements must be keyboard-navigable

## Brand Voice & Tone

### Core Voice Attributes
| Attribute | Description |
|---|---|
| **Intelligent** | Smart, substantive, never dumbed-down |
| **Warm** | Human, community-oriented, inclusive |
| **Bold** | Confident, clear, not timid or bureaucratic |
| **Authentic** | Genuine, grounded in real Dartmouth stories |

### Tone by Context
- **Formal (admissions, academic):** Precise, elevated, inspiring
- **Conversational (social, student-facing):** Friendly, energetic, accessible
- **Crisis / Institutional:** Clear, calm, authoritative, empathetic

### Writing Style
- Use active voice
- Avoid jargon and acronyms without definition
- Write for a general educated audience unless context demands otherwise
- Dartmouth (not "Dartmouth College" in every instance after first reference)
- Refer to the **Dartmouth Editorial Style Guide** for specific AP/institutional style rules
